# Rulebook: AHV/AVS for newcomers to HSG (Swiss, EU/EFTA, UK, other countries)

Version 2, 10 Oct 2026 (deep-search edition: adds the federal law text, the official refund directive, 9 country agreement leaflets, back-payments, pension effects, self-employment and military EO). Built from official pages only (Fedlex law texts, ahv-iv.ch leaflets, BSV, ZAS, SVA St.Gallen, HSG). Every row quotes the source in its original language; quotes are short exact fragments because the page extractor caps quote length. "not in source" means the cited page does not say it; no gaps were filled from general knowledge. Where a row combines two sources into a conclusion, it says "inferred". Before Demo Day, spot-check a few quotes against the live page.

AHV = Alters- und Hinterlassenenversicherung (FR: AVS, EN: OASI). Students pay one combined contribution for AHV/IV/EO (old age, invalidity, income compensation).

**Profiles used in the "Who" column**
- **All**: any student at HSG, any nationality.
- **Domiciled**: civil-law domicile (zivilrechtlicher Wohnsitz) in Switzerland. Typical for degree students who moved here.
- **Study-only**: in Switzerland only to study, with no civil-law domicile here. Typical for exchange students; the compensation office decides case by case.
- **Working**: paid job, assistantship or internship in Switzerland.
- **Cross-border**: works or lives in more than one country (incl. remote work for a foreign employer, commuters).
- **Swiss**, **EU/EFTA**, **UK**, **Agreement country** (non-EU/EFTA state with a social security agreement), **Non-agreement country**.

**Key ages**
- Working: contributions from 1 January after the **17th** birthday.
- Not working: from 1 January after the **20th** birthday (= the year you turn 21).
- Student flat rate (CHF 530) up to 31 December of the year you turn **25**; from 1 January after the 25th birthday, contributions depend on assets and pension income.

## 2a. The law behind the student rules

These are the federal provisions the leaflets summarise. Useful for the website's "why" tooltips and for disputes with SVA.

## 8. Short summaries per profile (for the website)

**Swiss student, living in St.Gallen.** From the year you turn 21 you pay CHF 530 a year (plus up to 5 % admin cost) until the year you turn 25. SVA St.Gallen emails you a questionnaire each April/May for the year before. If your job already paid CHF 530 in AHV, you pay nothing extra; if less, only the difference. Your AHV number is on your health insurance card. Going abroad for your studies? You can keep paying Swiss AHV until the year you turn 30. (R1–R8, R11–R13, P1, N1, L10)

**EU/EFTA degree student who moved to St.Gallen.** Same rules as Swiss students: your nationality gives no exemption. If you work across the border or remotely for an employer in another country, only one country's social security applies; check with the employer or SVA before you start. If you leave Switzerland, you cannot get your AHV contributions back, but they count towards a pension later. (R1, C1–C5, L1–L2)

**EU/EFTA exchange student (one or two semesters).** If you are in Switzerland only to study and your domicile stays at home, you normally pay no student AHV. SVA may still send you the questionnaire; answer it and explain your situation. (R9, P1–P2)

**UK student.** Same Swiss rules. For cross-border work, the CH–UK agreement applies (fully in force since 1 Oct 2023). The official refund directive lists no refund for UK nationals, so plan on contributions staying in the AHV and counting towards a pension. (R1, C6, L7)

**Student from another country (e.g. USA, India, China, Brazil).** Same Swiss rules: domiciled here and 21+ means CHF 530 a year. If your country has no agreement with Switzerland, you can claim AHV contributions back after leaving for good with at least one year paid (bring your deregistration certificate). Australia, Brazil, China, India, the Philippines, South Korea, Tunisia and Uruguay also allow refunds; for the USA, Canada, Japan and the UK no refund is listed, but your Swiss pension is paid abroad later. (R1, C8–C10, L3–L9, country table)

**Working student (any nationality).** Your employer deducts 5.3 % AHV/IV/EO and 1.1 % unemployment insurance from your wage from the year after you turn 17. On a small job (up to CHF 2,500 a year per employer) nothing is deducted unless you ask, except babysitting/household and arts jobs. (W1–W8)

**Over 25 and not working.** The student flat rate ends; SVA calculates your contribution from assets and pension income (CHF 530 to 26,500 a year). If your parents support you, the law says you may still pay only the minimum; SVA decides. (R14–R16, G7)

**Everyone: why paying matters.** Each missing year cuts your future Swiss pension by 1/44, and unpaid years can only be paid within 5 years. Order a free account extract every few years and dispute errors within 30 days. (B1–B9)

**Commuter living in Germany/Austria, studying at HSG.** Studying in St.Gallen alone does not create a Swiss domicile, so no student AHV is due; a job in Switzerland is still subject to Swiss AHV. (P9, G2, W1)
